# Running rituals

Mechanics live in [../game-systems/rituals.md](../game-systems/rituals.md). This page is only
about how to spend a ritual well.

## Enter at the highest level your loadout can win

Starting level scales the payout, not just the price. E.g. Fundamentals, at the same 15 Zeal and
the same `03:10` duration each time: `Lv 2` paid +2.69e131 Ceremony/s and 8.31e163 Ritual Xp,
`Lv 200` paid +1.45e201 and 1.13e240, `Lv 240` paid +5.32e215 and 3.26e255. The completion cost
rose with it (2.55e-21 → 1.05e166 → 2.47e223 Knowledge) but stayed a sliver of the Knowledge on
hand.

The rule: enter at the highest level that still finishes. Your other rituals' levels are the guide:
a ritual whose own track lags the rest can jump straight to where they sit, and a low run first
only wastes Zeal. A run that stalls pays nothing for the ambition, so step up from a level you have won,
not from guesswork.

The winnable level is per ritual, because each ritual rolls its own enemy mix. Judge it in the first
wave: compare the enemies' health with what your spell does to a plain enemy, not with the biggest
number on screen. When the plain hit is at or above that health, the run finishes in a handful of
casts. When it is a few orders of magnitude short, or the wave is full of shielded enemies, the run
stalls, so quit it at once and bisect down.

Only frontier runs move the Ritual level. A run a dozen levels below your best pays about a millionth
of the Ritual Xp bar, so a sweep of lower rituals buys their duration rewards and nothing else.

## Never press End Ritual on a run you can still win

End Ritual is the give-up button. It scores the run `Ritual failed.` regardless of how many waves
you cleared, which forfeits the **New Level Achieved** line and the duration reward that only a
completed run grants. You keep the spoils for the waves you cleared, and nothing else.

So it is worth exactly one thing: cutting a **stalled** run — one where a wave's health pool is out
of reach and the completion cost is draining for nothing. Everything else, let it finish; a won run
ends by itself and opens the same panel with more on it. Cutting a stall costs nothing beyond its
Zeal: the Ritual Chain survives it.

## The bottleneck is attack rate, so cast

Auto-attack will clear a ritual well inside your power without any input, but it fires roughly once
every second or two at a single target, and every wave respawns a fresh set. Left alone, a trivial
five-wave run still grinds for minutes.

Prefer a **multi-hit** spell over a big single hit here: a spell that lands five attacks per cast
converts a minutes-long grind into a handful of casts. Well inside your power, damage per hit is
rarely the constraint; the number of enemies you can touch per second is. At the frontier the
auto-attack's damage falls out of the fight entirely, and the run is won only by pressing an instant
multi-target spell, cast after cast.

## Overlapping rituals get more expensive to enter

The Zeal price beside Activate rises while previous runs' duration rewards are still ticking (see
[../game-systems/rituals.md](../game-systems/rituals.md)). Zeal regenerates against a small cap, so stacking
rituals back to back is self-limiting. If the goal is uptime on one buff, re-enter as it expires
rather than layering runs; if the goal is the level track, spend the Zeal deliberately and expect
the next entry to cost more.
